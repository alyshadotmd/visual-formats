# UGC Format — Implementation SOP

## Reference examples (look here first)

To find examples, look in the shared `../../files/` folder for any file whose name contains `vf=ugc`.

**Study a few first** — notice the phone-camera feel, the creator's own space, and how little it resembles a brand production.

## What this format is

A **fallback** tag for user-generated-style content: a creator-style video that is clearly UGC but doesn't match any specific UGC format in this library. Specific UGC formats (`yapper`, `testimonial`, `interview`, `street-interview`, `ugc-mashup`, `unboxing`, `taste-test` and the rest) always win when they fit, so this format only applies once those have been ruled out. It works because creator content reads as a real person sharing something, not a brand pitching it, which lowers the viewer's ad-guard the way any native-feeling content does.

Strongest for **general product-in-use moments that don't fit a named UGC shape** — a quick day-in-the-life mention, a casual recommendation, a light demo.

The one property to protect: **it has to look like creator content, not a brand production.** A real-seeming person, phone-camera feel, and their own space are what sell the format.

## Inputs

Expect a **messaging angle** and the **product moment** the creator should show (used in their day, a quick recommendation, a casual demo). The angle sets what the video needs to land; this SOP governs the creator-native execution.

## Anatomy

1. **The creator:** a real-seeming person in their own space (home, kitchen, workplace), not a studio set.
2. **The product in use:** shown being used naturally within the creator's day, not staged as a product shot.
3. **The edit:** native-feeling social cuts, phone footage, simple captions, light b-roll.

## Build rules (priority order)

1. **Cast a real-seeming creator in their own space.**
2. **Show the product in use in their day.**
3. **Keep the edit native to social** (phone footage, simple cuts, captions).
4. **Reclassify when it becomes a clear specific format.** If the video turns into a clear yap, a testimonial arc, or another named UGC format, brief it as that format instead.
5. **Specific, real claims; no absolutes or banned phrases.**

## Length caps

| Element | Cap |
|---|---|
| Captions | on for any talking, kept short |

## Build steps

1. Read the brief: angle, the product moment to show.
2. Cast a real-seeming creator whose space and voice fit the moment.
3. Film the product in use naturally within their day.
4. Edit natively: phone footage, simple cuts, light b-roll, captions on.
5. Check whether the result now fits a specific UGC format (yapper, testimonial, etc.); if so, re-brief as that format.
6. Claims pass (validate anything claimed or demonstrated).
7. Deliver: creator/moment brief, shot list, edit direction, claims flags.

## QA checklist
- [ ] It looks like creator content, not a brand production: a real-seeming person, phone-camera feel, their own space
- [ ] A person is on camera for a meaningful part of it (talking, using the product)
- [ ] No specific UGC format fits cleanly
- [ ] Honest claims; no absolutes or banned phrases
