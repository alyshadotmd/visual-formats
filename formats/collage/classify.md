# Collage — Classification Guide

(Renamed from `gift-guide` by Alysha, 2026-09-30. These ads are collages; calling them gift guides implied a gifting frame they don't need.)

## Reference examples (look here first)

To find examples, look in the shared `../../files/` folder for any file whose name contains `vf=collage`.

## Definition

A static built as a **collage of separate cut-outs** on one canvas. Clipped product shots sit loosely alongside lifestyle and accessory images (clips, bows, brushes, jewelry, people shot from behind) on a plain background, at mixed sizes and often overlapping. It has the look of a Pinterest board or an Instagram-story picks page, so it reads as curated and shareable rather than as a brand layout. A short title ("Black Friday Sale Picks") and a small shop button are common but not required.

## The things that must all be true

1. **Collage of separate cut-outs**, not one photo. Products and props are clipped out and placed individually.
2. **Loose, editorial placement.** Items scatter and overlap; there is no grid or boxed tiles.
3. **Mixed items.** The collage usually includes things that aren't the product (accessories, styling, people) to build a mood or persona.

## Quick test

Does it look like someone cut things out and pasted them together on one page, with no cells? If yes, Collage.

## Commonly confused with

- **Bento-grid:** content in tidy, boxed tiles. A collage is loose and overlapping, with no grid.
- **Flatlay:** one overhead photo of real products arranged on a surface. A collage is a digital composite of separate cut-outs, often with people and props that aren't the product.
- **Product-grid:** identical, same-size cells, one SKU per slot. A collage is deliberately uneven.

## Signals

White or soft solid background, clipped product PNGs, hair or fashion accessories, a person from behind, a bold "Picks" title, a small pill-shaped shop button, little or no offer copy.
