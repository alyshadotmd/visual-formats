# Podcast Format — Implementation SOP

## Reference examples (look here first)

To find examples, look in the shared `../../files/` folder for any file whose name contains `vf=podcast`.

## What this format is

A video built from **clips made to look like a podcast recording**: people talking at podcast microphones, headphones, a podcast set or couch, often with a host and guest. The product comes up in the conversation like it would on a real show. It works because the podcast framing reads as an overheard, unscripted conversation rather than an ad, which lowers the viewer's guard before the pitch lands.

Strongest for **conversational, trust-building pitches and products that benefit from sounding recommended rather than sold.**

The one property to protect: **it has to read as a real clip from a conversation**, not a monologue staged in front of podcast props.

## Inputs

Expect a **messaging angle** (what the host or guest says about the product and why it comes up) and a sense of the **host/guest dynamic**. The angle supplies the talking point; this SOP governs the podcast staging and how the clip is cut.

## Anatomy

1. **The set:** a podcast mic on an arm or stand, headphones, a couch or two chairs, a lived-in podcast look.
2. **The opening hook:** a real-sounding moment pulled from the middle of a conversation, not an introduction.
3. **The product beat:** the product comes up naturally in the talk, as it would on a real show, not as a script-read ad break.
4. **The close:** either cuts to product and offer, or stays on the set for the whole ad.

## Build rules (priority order)

1. **Set the scene with real podcast gear.** Mics, headphones, a lived-in podcast set — not a generic interview backdrop.
2. **Open on a real-sounding moment** from the conversation, not an introduction or a pitch line.
3. **Let the product come up naturally in the talk,** as something recommended, not announced.
4. **Cut to product and offer, or keep the whole ad on the set** — whichever keeps the conversational tone intact.
5. **Specific, real claims. No absolutes or banned phrases.**

## Length caps

| Element | Cap |
|---|---|
| Opening hook clip | ≤10s |
| Product beat | Reads as a natural aside, not an extended ad break |

## Build steps

1. Read the brief: the talking point to carry and the host/guest dynamic.
2. Set the scene: podcast mics, headphones, a lived-in podcast set.
3. Write the opening hook as a real-sounding mid-conversation moment.
4. Write the product beat so it comes up naturally in the talk.
5. Decide whether to cut to product/offer or stay on the set for the full ad.
6. Claims pass (claims made in dialogue are real and specific).
7. Sound-off check: captions carry the key line if sound-off viewing is expected.
8. Deliver: opening hook line, product beat dialogue, cut decision, claims flags.

## QA checklist

- [ ] A podcast setup is visible: a podcast microphone on an arm or stand, headphones, a podcast-style set
- [ ] It plays as a clip from a conversation, not a talk straight to the viewer
- [ ] The podcast clip is a real part of the ad, often the hook
- [ ] The product comes up naturally, not as a scripted ad break
- [ ] Honest claims; no absolutes or banned phrases
