# Split Screen — Classification Guide

(Named by Alysha, 2026-09-30, from the BFCM swipe file review. Media: usually video.)

## Definition

The frame is **split into two side-by-side panels that run at the same time**. Usually one panel is a fixed, designed panel (logo, product shot, the offer) and the other plays live footage (a creator talking, product swatches, a demo). The viewer gets the pitch and the proof at once.

## The things that must all be true

1. **Two panels share the frame**, side by side (or top and bottom), for most of the ad.
2. **Both panels stay on screen together.** It's one layout, not a cut from one shot to another.
3. **The panels do different jobs**, for example a fixed offer panel next to moving footage.

## Quick test

Pause anywhere in the middle. Do you see two separate panels at once? If yes, Split Screen.

## Commonly confused with

- **Before and After:** two matched shots of the same thing, before vs after. Split Screen panels don't need to match.
- **Us vs Them:** a comparison of the brand against a competitor. Split Screen isn't a comparison.
- **Offer Banner:** a single designed frame where the offer is the hero. When the offer sits in its own panel next to live footage, it's Split Screen.
- **Bento Grid / Collage:** many tiles. Split Screen is exactly two panels.

## Signals

A narrow white or brand-color panel with logo, "Black Friday Sale" and the % off, next to a creator video or close-up product footage; the offer panel stays fixed while the footage plays.

## Examples

- Honeylove, "3 mistakes I used to make with shapewear" (creator video right, product and "30-60% off" left).
- Jones Road Beauty, "Black Friday Sale, 20% off orders 100+" (offer panel left, product swatches right).
