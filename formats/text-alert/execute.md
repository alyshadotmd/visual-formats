# Text Alert Format — Implementation SOP

## Reference examples (look here first)

To find examples, look in the shared `../../files/` folder for any file whose name contains `vf=text-alert`.

## What this format is

A video where a **phone notification** (an iMessage or app banner) drops into an everyday moment; the person taps it and the ad cuts to the product and the offer. It works because a real-looking notification feels like something that happened to the viewer, not an ad interrupting them, so the cut to product lands as a discovery rather than a pitch.

Strongest for **offer- and urgency-led hooks** — a sale alert, a restock, a limited-time drop — where "you just got this text" is the premise.

The one property to protect: **the notification has to read as a real, native phone alert**, not an obvious graphic overlay.

## Inputs

Expect a **messaging angle** (the offer or hook the notification delivers) and the everyday moment it should interrupt. The angle supplies the banner's line and the offer; this SOP governs the notification staging and the cut.

## Anatomy

1. **The everyday moment:** someone mid-task, or a phone lock screen, setting up the interruption.
2. **The notification banner:** an iMessage or app banner dropping in with the hook line.
3. **The tap:** the person taps the notification.
4. **The cut to product and offer:** lands on the product and the offer the notification promised.

## Build rules (priority order)

1. **Open on an everyday moment or lock screen** so the notification reads as a real interruption.
2. **Drop in a banner with the hook line,** styled like a native iMessage or app notification.
3. **Tap, then cut to the product and offer.**
4. **Keep the notification looking native,** not like an obvious graphic overlay.
5. **Specific, real claims. No absolutes or banned phrases.**

## Length caps

| Element | Cap |
|---|---|
| Banner hook line | ≤10 words |
| Everyday-moment setup | ≤5s before the banner drops |

## Build steps

1. Read the brief: the offer or hook the notification should deliver.
2. Write the everyday moment or lock screen the notification will interrupt.
3. Write the banner hook line.
4. Stage the banner drop-in and the tap, keeping the notification native-looking.
5. Cut to the product and the offer.
6. Claims pass (offer terms accurate; no absolutes or banned phrases).
7. Legibility check at thumb size.
8. Deliver: everyday-moment setup, banner line, cut timing, product/offer shot, claims flags.

## QA checklist

- [ ] A notification banner appears on screen
- [ ] It interrupts a moment (someone mid-task, or a phone lock screen)
- [ ] It leads into the product or offer
- [ ] The notification reads as a native phone alert, not a graphic overlay
- [ ] Honest claims; no absolutes or banned phrases
