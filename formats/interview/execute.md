# Interview Format — Implementation SOP

## Reference examples (look here first)

To find examples, look in the shared `../../files/` folder for any file whose name contains `vf=interview`.

## What this format is

A video built around interviewing people about the product: an interviewer asks questions and one or more people answer on camera about their experience with it. It works because hearing a real person answer a real question reads as more credible than a pitch delivered straight to camera — the interviewer's presence signals the answer wasn't scripted by the brand.

Strongest for **testimonial-style proof in a planned, intentional setting, and multiple-perspective social proof** when several interviewees are used.

The one property to protect: **the interviewer's questions have to visibly or audibly drive it, and the setting has to read as planned, not strangers stopped in public.** Lose either and it drifts into street interview or testimonial.

## Inputs

Expect a **messaging angle** — the benefits the questions should surface — plus the interviewee(s) and the planned setting. The angle governs the benefits the answers need to land; this SOP governs the question set and the camera/cut.

## Anatomy

1. **The interviewer:** asks questions, heard, seen, or shown as text.
2. **The interviewee(s):** one or more people answering on camera about the product.
3. **The setting:** planned, not strangers stopped in public.
4. **The offer/close:** where the cut lands.

## Build rules (priority order)
1. **Write 3-5 questions that lead to the product's benefits.**
2. **Film real answers; keep the questions visible or audible.**
3. **Cut the best answers into a story; end on the offer.**
4. **Keep the setting planned and intentional**, not a street stop.
5. **Honest claims.** No absolutes or banned phrases.

## Length caps

| Element | Cap |
|---|---|
| Questions | 3-5 |
| Interviewees | 1 or more |

## Build steps

1. Read the brief: the benefits the questions should surface.
2. Write 3-5 questions that lead toward those benefits.
3. Cast the interviewee(s) and set the planned setting.
4. Film the interview, keeping the questions visible or audible.
5. Cut the best answers into a story.
6. End on the offer.
7. Claims pass: claims specific and validated, no absolutes or banned phrases.
8. Deliver: question list, interviewee notes, cut order, offer copy, claims flags.

## QA checklist
- [ ] An interviewer's questions drive it (heard, seen or shown as text)
- [ ] People answer on camera about the product
- [ ] A planned setting, not strangers stopped in public
- [ ] Honest claims; no absolutes or banned phrases
