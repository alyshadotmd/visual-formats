# Unexpected Text Placement Format — Implementation SOP

## Reference examples (look here first)

To find examples, look in the shared `../../files/` folder for any file whose name contains `vf=unexpected-text-placement`.

## What this format is

The ad's message written straight onto a surprising surface: a body, the ground, food. The surprise of where the words sit stops the scroll. It works best when the surface ties to the benefit (a stomach for a gut product, a birthday cake for a gift box, the street for an offer you "stumble on").

Strongest for **scroll-stopping hooks where the surface itself can echo the benefit** — body-related claims, food or treat offers, and "found" or surprise-style offers.

The one property to protect: **the words look like they're really on that surface**, and they stay readable.

## Inputs

Expect a **messaging angle** (the line to write) and a **surface** that connects to it — a body for a body benefit, food for a treat, the ground for a "found" deal. The angle supplies the line; this SOP governs how and where it's rendered.

## Anatomy

1. **The surface:** an unexpected place for words to live — a body, the ground, food — chosen because it ties to the message.
2. **The written line:** the ad's message or offer, rendered in the surface's own medium (marker on skin, stencil or paint on stone, piped icing on a cake).
3. **Brand/product in view:** visible in the shot or named in the line, so it isn't mistaken for a random photo.
4. **Results disclaimer (where relevant):** added when the claim needs it.

## Build rules (priority order)

1. **Pick a surface that connects to the message** (body for body benefits, food for treats, the ground for "found" deals).
2. **Make the text belong to the surface:** marker on skin, stencil or paint on stone, icing on a cake. Match the texture and curve.
3. **One short line or two,** readable at thumb size.
4. **Brand and product in view or in the line** so it isn't mistaken for a random photo.
5. **Add a results disclaimer** where claims warrant it.
6. **Real, specific claims. No absolutes or banned phrases.**

## Length caps

| Element | Cap |
|---|---|
| Main line | ≤12 words |
| Offer line | 1 short line |

## Build steps

1. Read the brief: the angle's line and any benefit the surface should echo.
2. Pick the surface that connects to the message.
3. Write the line to fit the surface (short enough to render by hand or by prop).
4. Render the text in the surface's own medium, matching its texture and curve.
5. Make sure brand or product is in view or named in the line.
6. Add a results disclaimer where the claim warrants it.
7. Claims pass (specific, real; validate any numbers).
8. Legibility check at thumb size.
9. Deliver: surface choice, line, rendering note, disclaimer, claims flags.

## QA checklist

- [ ] The words sit on the surface, not over the photo
- [ ] The surface is surprising and ties to the message
- [ ] Legible at thumb size
- [ ] Brand or product is in view or named
- [ ] Results disclaimer present where claims warrant it
- [ ] Claims specific and validated; no absolutes or banned phrases
