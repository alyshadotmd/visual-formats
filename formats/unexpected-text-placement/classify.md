# Unexpected Text Placement — Classification Guide

(Named by Alysha, 2026-09-30. Replaces the old Written On Body and AI Generated Static formats.)

## Reference examples (look here first)

To find examples, look in the shared `../../files/` folder for any file whose name contains `vf=unexpected-text-placement`.

## Definition

A static where the ad's message is **written directly onto a surface where you would never expect to read an ad**:
a person's stomach or arm, a sidewalk or cement floor, the top of a cake. The surprise of *where* the words sit is
what stops the scroll. The text looks like it belongs to the surface (marker on skin, paint or stencil on stone,
icing on a cake), not like a caption laid on top.

## The things that must all be true

1. **The core message sits on a physical surface in the scene**, not in a caption, sticker or banner over the photo.
2. **The surface is unexpected for words** (a body, a floor, food, nature). A sign, a sheet of paper, a whiteboard or
   a sticky note is where words normally go, so those are their own formats.
3. **The text takes on the surface's look** (handwriting on skin, weathered paint on concrete, piped icing).

It doesn't matter whether the photo is real, staged or AI-made. What counts is where the text sits.

## Quick test

Would you be surprised to find words written there in real life? If yes, Unexpected Text Placement.

## Commonly confused with

- **Instagram Text Overlay:** digital text or stickers placed over a photo.
- **Post-it / Whiteboard / Letter / Flyer / Sign:** words on a surface made for words.
- **Ad in the Wild:** a designed billboard or poster placed in a street scene. Unexpected Text Placement is written onto
  the surface itself (the floor, not a poster on the wall).

## Signals

Marker on skin, stencil or paint on pavement, icing lettering, text following the curve or texture of what it's on.
