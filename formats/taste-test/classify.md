# Taste Test — Classification Guide

## Definition

A video whose hero is **one or more products being consumed on camera and the immediate sensory reaction to the taste** (or lack of taste). The tasting beat is the argument: the reaction, the verdict, or the "you can't taste it" reveal is what sells the product. Includes both the standard variant (a creator tastes a product and reacts to the flavor) and the flavorless-demo variant (a creator adds a product to a drink to prove it doesn't change the taste).

## The things that must all be true

1. **A product is consumed on camera** — sipped, chewed, mixed into a drink and drunk, spooned into the mouth.
2. **The taste (or the null taste) is the argument** — the reaction beat is what the ad is asking the viewer to evaluate, not a side moment inside a broader monologue.
3. **The reaction is on camera** — a facial reaction, a spoken verdict ("oh that's actually good," "I can't taste anything"), or a comparison to another product tasted in the same clip.

## Quick test

Ask: *is the moment of tasting, and the reaction to that taste, the point of the ad?* If yes, Taste Test. If the tasting is one beat inside a broader talking-head pitch or demo, it's the other format with a taste beat, not a Taste Test.

## Variants

- **Flavor reaction (standard).** Creator tastes a product and reacts to the flavor. Usually a positive "oh wow" or a comparison beat.
- **Flavor comparison / ranking.** Creator tastes multiple flavors, variants, or brands in sequence and ranks them or picks a favorite.
- **Flavorless demo.** Creator adds an unflavored product (drops, powder, supplement) to a drink and drinks it to prove it doesn't change the taste. The "null" reaction is the proof.
- **Blind test.** Creator tastes without knowing which is which, then reveals the pick.

## Commonly confused with

- **yapper:** fast talking-head monologue with no tasting beat, or where the tasting beat is incidental (creator holds a drink but the ad is about ingredients). Yapper carries taste-adjacent talk; Taste Test carries the actual tasting.
- **unboxing:** opens or reveals a product but doesn't consume it on camera as the argument.
- **review:** review cards or a pulled customer quote presented graphically. Taste Test is the creator's own live tasting reaction, not a graphic review layout.
- **testimonial:** full struggle → turning point → result arc narrated by the user. Taste Test is short and centered on the tasting moment, not a life-story arc.
- **feature-benefit-pointout:** annotates a product with call-outs. Taste Test uses live consumption and reaction as the argument, not graphic annotations.

## Signals to look for

- Cup, glass, spoon, or dropper visibly used to consume the product
- Immediate on-camera reaction (facial, verbal, or a "let me try that again")
- On-screen text calling out flavor language ("actually tastes like…", "unflavored", "0 sugar and it slaps")
- For the flavorless-demo variant: the creator sips the same drink before and after adding the product, or squeezes/pours the product in on camera and immediately drinks
- For the ranking variant: numbered items or brand labels tasted in sequence with a verdict per item

## What does NOT count

- A creator holding a drink while explaining ingredients but never tasting on camera.
- A product demo where the creator uses the product on skin/hair/surface (that's the relevant application format, not Taste Test).
- A voiceover-only ad describing taste with no on-camera consumption.
