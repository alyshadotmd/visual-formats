# Taste Test Format — Implementation SOP

## Reference examples (look here first)

To find examples, look in the shared `../../files/` folder for any file whose name contains `vf=taste-test`.

**Study the tasting beat first** — how the consumption is framed, when the reaction lands, how the product enters the drink or the mouth.

## What this format is

A short video where consuming the product on camera, and the sensory reaction to that consumption, is the primary argument. It works because taste is one of the few claims a viewer can't verify from a graphic — a real reaction on camera compresses the credibility gap between "this tastes good" and the viewer's own experience. It also works for the inverse: proving a supplement or drop is truly flavorless is far more convincing when someone sips a familiar drink, adds the product, and sips again.

Strongest for **flavor-driven categories (drinks, food, powders, drops, sweets), ingestible supplements where taste is a common objection, and "does it actually taste good?" awareness stages.** Not the right choice for products you don't consume, or for taste claims that need long-form context to land.

The one property to protect: **the tasting beat and the reaction must be on camera and unambiguous.** If the reaction is off-camera, cut, or hedged, the format collapses into a demo or a talking head.

### Common variants

- **Flavor reaction (standard).** Single product, one tasting beat, one reaction — usually a positive "oh wow" or a comparison beat.
- **Flavor comparison / ranking.** Multiple flavors or brands tasted in sequence with per-item verdicts and a final pick. Faster cuts, tighter reactions.
- **Flavorless demo.** Unflavored drops or powder added to a drink; sip-add-sip to isolate the "no change" verdict as the proof.
- **Blind test.** Creator tastes without knowing which is which, ranks or picks, then reveals. Higher trust when the reveal is unambiguous and couldn't have been edited in after the fact.

## Inputs

Expect a **messaging angle** (the specific taste claim to prove: "tastes like a mocha, not a supplement", "0 sugar and still tastes like Coke", "unflavored, mixes into anything"), persona and awareness stage, the **product's actual taste profile and any comparison claims that need clearance**, and VOC on how buyers describe the taste in their own words. The angle picks the claim; the tasting beat proves it; this SOP governs how that beat is built.

If the taste claim can't be proven in a single on-camera reaction, split it into multiple ads or pick a different format.

## Anatomy

1. **The setup:** one short line naming the claim being proved ("this is supposed to taste like a milkshake — let's see," "these drops are unflavored, watch," "I'm ranking the 5 electrolyte flavors"). Sets the viewer's expectation for what the tasting beat is testing.
2. **The consumption beat:** the product enters the mouth on camera, visibly — sip, spoon, bite, or squeeze-into-drink-and-sip. Frame tight enough that the consumption is unambiguous. For the flavorless-demo variant: sip the drink, add the product on camera, sip again.
3. **The reaction beat:** immediate facial and verbal reaction ("Oh that's actually good," "I genuinely can't taste anything," "8/10 for me"). The reaction must be legible even sound-off — face carries the verbal reaction when captions are on, and the caption carries the verdict.
4. **The reveal / product name:** product on screen with brand and name once the reaction has landed. Hold the product name until after the tasting beat, except for ranking variants where each item is named as it's tasted.
5. **CTA (optional):** a short offer or shop link after the reveal. The tasting beat is what earned the click; the CTA just captures it.

## Build rules (priority order)

1. **The tasting is on camera or the ad doesn't ship.** No off-camera sipping, no cutting away at the moment of consumption.
2. **The reaction is immediate and legible.** Face + one clear line. No delayed cut to a talking-head overview.
3. **Name the claim in the setup.** The viewer needs to know what the tasting beat is testing before the sip happens, or the reaction has nothing to prove.
4. **Product name lands after the reaction, not before.** Exception: ranking variants where each item is named on entry.
5. **For flavorless demos, do the sip-add-sip sequence.** A single sip after adding is weaker than a before/after sip that isolates the change.
6. **Captions carry the verdict.** For sound-off delivery, the reaction line and verdict need to be on-screen text, not just spoken audio.
7. **Real, defensible claims.** The taste claim has to be one the brand can stand behind, with honest reactions — no hedged lines read as positive, no staged-looking reactions, no blind-test reveals edited in a way that couldn't have happened live.

## Length caps

| Element | Cap |
|---|---|
| Setup line | ≤10 words |
| Reaction line | ≤8 words, punchy |
| Number of tasted items in a ranking | 3-5 |

## Build steps

1. Read the brief: the taste claim to prove, persona and awareness stage, the product's actual taste profile, any comparison claims needing clearance, and VOC on how buyers describe the taste.
2. Pick the variant (flavor reaction, ranking, flavorless demo, or blind test) that fits the claim.
3. Write the setup line naming the claim being tested.
4. Shoot the consumption beat on camera, framed so it's unambiguous (sip-add-sip for flavorless demos).
5. Capture the immediate reaction beat — a clear facial and verbal reaction.
6. Reveal the product with brand and name after the reaction lands (name items on entry for ranking variants).
7. Add captions carrying the reaction line and verdict for sound-off viewing.
8. Add the CTA if the ad uses one.
9. Claims pass: the taste claim is one the brand can defend, comparison claims are cleared, reactions read as real.
10. Legibility / sound-off check: the verdict reads from captions alone.
11. Deliver: variant used, setup line, consumption and reaction beat notes, reveal timing, captions, CTA, claims flags.

## QA checklist

- [ ] Tasting happens on camera, not off-camera or cut away
- [ ] The claim being tested is named in the setup before the tasting beat
- [ ] The reaction is immediate and legible, on camera, with captions carrying the verdict
- [ ] Product name and brand land after the reaction (except ranking variants naming items on entry)
- [ ] Flavorless demos use the sip-add-sip sequence to isolate the "no change" verdict
- [ ] No hedged or staged-looking reactions, no taste claims the brand can't defend
- [ ] Honest, specific claims; no absolutes or banned phrases
