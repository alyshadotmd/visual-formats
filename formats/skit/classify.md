# Skit — Classification Guide

## Definition

A **scripted acted-out scene with defined characters and dialogue**, most often the same actress playing two roles in split-screen or cut-between shots. One character is "wrong," struggling, or misinformed; the other is "corrective," knowing, or coaching. The scene resolves in a product reveal or endorsement. The whole thing reads as a written mini-scene, not a monologue or a straight demo.

## The three things that must all be true

1. **There are two or more defined characters**, not just one person addressing the camera. Same actress playing both roles counts.
2. **The characters exchange dialogue in a scene** (voiced or captioned back-and-forth), not sequential talking-head lines.
3. **The scene is scripted with a setup and a resolution**, usually landing on the product as the resolution beat.

## Quick test

Ask: *is this a mini scene between two characters, and does the exchange between them carry the ad?* If yes, Skit. If it's one person talking to camera or a straight demo, it isn't.

## What does NOT count

- **A single talking head, even a lively one** — that's `yapper` or, with graphics popping around them, `graphic-anchor`.
- **A voiceover over b-roll** with no on-camera characters and no scripted exchange.
- **A user narrating their own story** to the camera — that's `testimonial`.
- **A comment-reply framing** where the "second voice" is a screenshotted comment, not an acted character — that's `comment-response`.
- **Two products or two panels labeled us vs them** with no characters or dialogue — that's `us-vs-them`.

## Commonly confused with

- **us-vs-them:** both hinge on contrast, but us-vs-them is a static or graphic side-by-side of two things. Skit is character-led and dialogue-led; the contrast lands through the scene between the characters, not through labeled panels.
- **yapper:** high-energy talking head. Skit has 2+ characters and a scripted exchange.
- **comment-response:** reply framing where the setup is a comment sticker. Skit's setup is a character acting out the problem, not a screenshotted comment.
- **founder:** the brand principal speaking as themselves. Skit uses fictional or role-played characters, not the founder as founder.
- **egc:** employee-created content. Skit is defined by the scripted scene shape, regardless of who filmed it.

## What to look for as supporting signals

- Split-screen composition, wardrobe/hair changes to distinguish characters, or clear cut-between shots
- A "wrong self + corrective self" pairing, or a patient/consultant, novice/expert, before/after-persona pairing
- A setup beat (character has a problem or misconception) → a corrective beat (other character introduces the product) → a resolution beat (wrong character comes around, tries it, converts)
- Text overlays that mirror the spoken dialogue between characters
