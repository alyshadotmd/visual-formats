# Live Demo Format — Implementation SOP

## Reference examples (look here first)

To find examples, look in the shared `../../files/` folder for any file whose name contains `vf=live-demo`.

## What this format is

A video where someone causes the problem on camera, and the product visibly reacts to it and fixes it, so the proof plays out live in front of you. It works because the viewer watches the test happen in one continuous shot, rather than being told or shown separate staged results — nothing is hidden between the "before" and the "after."

Strongest for **everyday, believable problems (household cleaner, coffee spill, a smudge) where the product's visible reaction — a reading, a light, a stain lifting, water beading off — is the proof**, and for demonstration-led products where watching the fix happen is more convincing than describing it.

The one property to protect: **the before-and-after has to happen in one continuous test, not as separate staged shots.** Split it into separate shots and it becomes a different format (before-and-after or transformation).

## Inputs

Expect a **messaging angle** — the problem/benefit argument — plus the specific real-world problem to create on camera (a spray, a spill, a smudge) and what visible reaction proves the fix (a reading, a light, a stain lifting, beading). The angle governs what's being proven; this SOP governs how the test is shot and paced.

## Anatomy

1. **The provocation:** the problem caused on purpose, on camera ("watch this, I'm gonna spray...").
2. **The product's visible reaction:** something you can see change (a reading, a light, a stain lifting, water beading off).
3. **The waiting-time fill:** a fast benefit run or lifestyle b-roll while the test plays out.
4. **The fix landing:** the product, surface, or reading back to normal.
5. **Product hero and CTA.**

### Beats (in order)
1. Open on the provocation as the hook: "Watch this" plus the action.
2. Name what's happening while it happens (e.g. "that color is gonna change").
3. Cut tight on the product's reaction (display, light, or surface) so it's readable on a phone.
4. Fill waiting time with a fast benefit run or lifestyle b-roll, then cut back to the result.
5. Close on the fix landing (back to normal), then the product hero and CTA.

## Build rules (priority order)
1. **Open on the provocation.** "Watch this" plus the action is the hook.
2. **Pick a problem that's everyday and believable** (household cleaner, coffee spill), not a lab stunt.
3. **Make the product's reaction readable on a phone.** Tight close-up on the display, light, or surface.
4. **Name what's happening while it happens.**
5. **Keep the before-and-after as one continuous test**, never separate staged shots.
6. **Fill the waiting time** with a fast benefit run or lifestyle b-roll, then cut back to the result.
7. **Close on the fix landing**, then the product hero and CTA.
8. **Honest claims.** No absolutes or banned phrases.

## Length caps

| Element | Cap |
|---|---|
| Test | 1 continuous take, no cutaways across it |
| Problem shown | 1, everyday and believable |

## Build steps

1. Read the brief: the problem/benefit argument and the specific everyday problem to create on camera.
2. Open on the provocation ("Watch this" plus the action) as the hook.
3. Shoot the product's visible reaction in a tight, phone-readable close-up.
4. Narrate what's happening while it happens.
5. Fill the waiting time with a fast benefit run or lifestyle b-roll.
6. Cut back to show the fix landing (back to normal).
7. Add the product hero and CTA.
8. Claims pass: claims specific and validated, no absolutes or banned phrases.
9. Deliver: provocation/problem chosen, shot list for the continuous test, voiceover line, b-roll fill, CTA, claims flags.

## QA checklist
- [ ] The test is real and the change is visible, not implied
- [ ] Hook is the provocation, not the logo
- [ ] Result is shown on camera before the end card
- [ ] Honest claims; no absolutes or banned phrases
