# Signature Series Format — Implementation SOP

## Reference examples (look here first)

To find examples, look in the shared `../../files/` folder for any file whose name contains `vf=signature-series`.

**Study one first** — notice how closely the paid episode matches the creator's unpaid ones in setup, joke style, and filming look.

## What this format is

A brand hires a creator to make one more episode of the creator's own recurring series — a repeat premise their audience already recognizes — done exactly the way they always do it (same setup, same bit, same voice), with the product written into the bit. It works because the episode doesn't read as an ad interrupting the feed; it reads as the thing the audience already follows the creator for, which carries the audience's existing trust and attention straight into the brand mention.

Strongest for **creators with an established recurring premise and audience, and brands that can genuinely fit inside that premise** rather than bolt onto it.

The one property to protect: **a follower has to recognize it as just another episode.** If it looks like a brand shoot wearing the creator's format, the format has failed.

## Inputs

Expect a **messaging angle** (how the product should be positioned inside the bit) and the **creator's existing series** to write it into. The angle decides what the product needs to say or prove; this SOP governs how the episode stays true to the creator's own series.

## Anatomy

1. **The established premise:** the creator's known recurring series, with its own setup and audience recognition, already running before this episode.
2. **The entry device:** the brand enters the way fans do, usually by requesting the episode in the creator's comments, so the brand shows up as an audience member rather than a sponsor.
3. **The bit itself:** the episode plays out exactly as the creator's unpaid episodes do — same setup, joke style, filming look, voice and timing — with the product written in as a prop, a punchline, or the premise. The series stays the hero.
4. **The offer (in caption, not the episode):** the code or offer sits in the caption, where the creator usually puts links, so the episode itself stays pure to the series.

## Build rules (priority order)

1. **Pick a creator whose series already fits the product**, so the product can become part of the joke or premise rather than being forced into it.
2. **Enter the series the way fans do.** Request the episode in the creator's comments, so the brand shows up as an audience member.
3. **The creator keeps full control** of the setup, filming style, voice and timing. Match their last unpaid episodes.
4. **The product shows up inside the bit** (as a prop, a punchline, the premise). The series stays the hero.
5. **Put the offer and code in the caption**, where the creator usually puts links, so the episode itself stays pure.
6. **Specific, real claims; no absolutes or banned phrases.**

| Element | Cap |
|---|---|
| Offer/code placement | caption only, not in the episode itself |

## Build steps

1. Read the brief: the angle, how the product should sit in the bit.
2. Confirm the creator has a known recurring series that genuinely fits the product.
3. Draft the entry device (usually a comment requesting the episode).
4. Brief the episode to match the creator's last unpaid episodes in setup, joke style, filming look, voice and timing.
5. Write the product into the bit as a prop, punchline, or part of the premise, keeping the series the hero.
6. Place the offer/code in the caption.
7. Claims pass (validate any claim made in the bit or caption).
8. Confirm partner disclosure is on.
9. Deliver: entry-device copy, episode brief matched to the creator's series, product placement note, caption offer copy, disclosure note, claims flags.

## QA checklist
- [ ] A follower would call it just another episode
- [ ] The product is part of the bit
- [ ] Partner disclosure is on (paid partnership label or #ad / #partner)
- [ ] Honest claims; no absolutes or banned phrases
