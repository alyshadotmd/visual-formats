# Feature Benefit Callout Format — Implementation SOP

## Reference examples (look here first)

To find examples, look in the shared `../../files/` folder for any file whose name contains `vf=feature-benefit-callout`.

**Study a few first** — notice how the benefit list sits around or beside the product with no arrows tying any label to a specific part of it.

## What this format is

A static where the product sits with a **list of its features or benefits** laid out around or beside it: icon rows, checkmark pills, short labeled lines. The list sells the product, but **nothing points at a specific part of it** — the benefits are about the whole product, not a part of it. It works because a scannable benefit list lets a viewer size up the whole value proposition in one glance, without needing to read a part-by-part breakdown.

Strongest for **broad value-proposition statics, "why this product" moments, and any product whose selling points are whole-product benefits rather than part-specific features.**

The one property to protect: **nothing points at a specific part of the product.** The moment a label is tied to one part by an arrow or line, it's Feature Benefit Pointout instead.

## Inputs

Expect a **messaging angle** and the **3-5 benefits** the angle wants to lead with. The angle decides which benefits matter most and how each is worded; this SOP governs the layout and list craft.

## Anatomy

1. **The product:** shown as the hero, usually held in hand or centered in frame.
2. **The headline (usual, not required):** one line over the product that frames the promise the list proves.
3. **The benefit list:** 3 or more features or benefits, each as an icon row, checkmark pill, or short labeled line, placed around or beside the product.
4. **Brand + CTA.**

## Build rules (priority order)

1. **Pick 3-5 benefits a buyer actually cares about**, each in 2-5 words.
2. **Give each one a simple icon or checkmark** so the list scans fast.
3. **Keep the product big and clear**; the list sits beside or around it, never crowding it out.
4. **Use one headline that frames the promise** the list proves.
5. **Keep the list whole-product, not part-specific.** No arrows or connector lines pointing at a specific part of the product — if a benefit belongs to a part, use Feature Benefit Pointout instead.
6. **Specific, real claims; no absolutes or banned phrases.**

## Length caps

| Element | Cap |
|---|---|
| Benefits listed | 3-5 |
| Words per benefit | 2-5 |
| Headline | ≤10 words |
| CTA | 1 |

## Build steps

1. Read the brief: angle, the benefits to lead with.
2. Choose 3-5 benefits a buyer cares about and word each in 2-5 words.
3. Pick a simple icon or checkmark per benefit.
4. Place the product as the hero; lay the list around or beside it without crowding it.
5. Write the headline (if used) that frames the promise.
6. Add brand + CTA.
7. Claims pass (validate every benefit claim; confirm no label points at a specific part).
8. Legibility check at thumb size.
9. Deliver: headline, benefit list with icons, layout note, claims flags.

## QA checklist
- [ ] The product is shown, usually as the hero or held in hand
- [ ] Several features or benefits are listed (3 or more), as icons, checkmarks or short lines
- [ ] No arrows or connector lines point at specific parts of the product
- [ ] Honest claims; no absolutes or banned phrases
