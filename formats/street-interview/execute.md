# Street Interview Format — Implementation SOP

## Reference examples (look here first)

To find examples, look in the shared `../../files/` folder for any file whose name contains `vf=street-interview`.

**Study a few first** — notice how the question or stunt is tied to the product and which reactions make the final cut.

## What this format is

A video where **people are stopped on the street** (or in public) and asked questions on camera, often a quiz, a taste test or a stunt, with the product woven in. It works because real, unscripted reactions from strangers read as more credible than a scripted testimonial — the viewer trusts what an unprepared person says on the spot more than a rehearsed line.

Strongest for **social proof through genuine reactions, quiz/trivia hooks, and taste or experience comparisons done in public.**

The one property to protect: **the people have to be real strangers reacting unscripted**, not actors reading lines. A staged-feeling interview loses the credibility the format depends on.

## Inputs

Expect a **messaging angle** (what the question or stunt is meant to prove about the product) and the **public setting and question/stunt concept**. The angle decides what to ask; this SOP governs how the interviews are run and filmed.

## Anatomy

1. **The public setting:** a street, mall, or event where strangers can be approached.
2. **The host with a mic:** stops passers-by and runs the question or stunt.
3. **The question or stunt:** tied to the product (a quiz, a taste test, a dare).
4. **The reactions:** the best, most genuine responses, cut together.
5. **The product/offer landing:** where the product and any offer are revealed at the end.

## Build rules (priority order)

1. **Pick a question or stunt tied to the product.**
2. **Film many strangers;** keep the best reactions.
3. **Land the product and offer at the end.**
4. **Keep the setting genuinely public** and the subjects genuinely unprepared strangers, not actors.
5. **Specific, real claims; no absolutes or banned phrases.**

## Length caps

| Element | Cap |
|---|---|
| Strangers filmed | many, to allow a strong-reactions cut |
| Product/offer landing | 1, at the end |

## Build steps

1. Read the brief: angle, what the question or stunt should prove about the product.
2. Design the question or stunt and pick a public setting.
3. Film many strangers reacting to the question or stunt.
4. Select the strongest, most genuine reactions.
5. Cut to land the product and offer at the end.
6. Claims pass (validate anything claimed by the host or implied by the reactions).
7. Deliver: question/stunt brief, setting note, selected-reactions cut direction, product/offer landing, claims flags.

## QA checklist
- [ ] Public setting (street, mall, event)
- [ ] Strangers stopped and asked on camera
- [ ] The product is woven into the questions or stunt
- [ ] Honest claims; no absolutes or banned phrases
