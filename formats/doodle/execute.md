# Doodle Format — Implementation SOP

## Reference examples (look here first)

To find examples, look in the shared `../../files/` folder for any file whose name contains `vf=doodle`.

## What this format is

An original hand-drawn or line-drawn character or scene (often lightly animated) carries the ad, with a caption on top. It works because an original, hand-drawn feel reads as a relatable post rather than an ad, so a familiar thought lands softer and more personal than it would over a photo.

Strongest for **relatable, meme-adjacent hooks and lighter-touch brand moments** where a drawn, un-polished feel builds warmth rather than needing a photo-real proof point.

The one property to protect: **the drawing is the brand's own original art**, not a recognizable meme template or a photo with lettering added.

## Inputs

Expect a **messaging angle** written as a relatable thought or joke, and a sense of what **character or scene** should carry it. The angle supplies the caption; this SOP governs the drawing and any light animation.

## Anatomy

1. **The drawing:** an original hand-drawn or line-drawn character or scene, the main visual of the frame.
2. **The caption:** the relatable thought or joke, written like a real post.
3. **Light animation (optional):** a blink, a tear, a small loop — kept simple.
4. **Brand/offer close:** one message, one offer or CTA.

## Build rules (priority order)

1. **Keep the drawing simple and expressive;** one character, one feeling.
2. **Write the caption like a relatable post,** not ad copy.
3. **If it moves, keep it light:** a blink, a tear, a small loop.
4. **One message, one offer or CTA.**
5. **Specific, real claims. No absolutes or banned phrases.**

## Length caps

| Element | Cap |
|---|---|
| Caption | ≤1-2 short lines |
| Animation loop (if used) | A single simple motion |

## Build steps

1. Read the brief: the relatable thought or joke the angle wants to land.
2. Sketch the character or scene that carries one clear feeling.
3. Write the caption in a relatable, post-like voice.
4. Add light animation only if it strengthens the moment (a blink, a tear, a small loop).
5. Add the brand mark and one offer or CTA.
6. Claims pass (any claim in the caption is specific and validated).
7. Legibility check at thumb size.
8. Deliver: drawing description, caption copy, animation note, CTA, claims flags.

## QA checklist

- [ ] The device carries the message
- [ ] The drawing is original, not a meme template or lettering over a photo
- [ ] One character, one feeling; the caption reads like a relatable post
- [ ] It reads at a glance on a phone
- [ ] Honest claims; no absolutes or banned phrases
