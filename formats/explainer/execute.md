# Explainer Format — Implementation SOP

## Reference examples (look here first)

To find examples, look in the shared `../../files/` folder for any file whose name contains `vf=explainer`.

## What this format is

A video where **someone who knows the topic explains something to you with confidence**. They may be a trusted expert, or just a knowledgeable everyday person. The ad teaches first (a comparison, a how-it-works, a myth cleared up), and the product fits into what you just learned.

Strongest for **myth-busting, comparison or how-it-works lessons, and category education** where the buyer currently overestimates or misunderstands something.

The one property to protect: **the content has to actually teach something** — strip the product out and the viewer should still have learned the lesson.

## Inputs

Expect a **messaging angle** (the misconception or lesson to teach), a teacher (an expert or a confident everyday person), and the props or visuals that show the lesson. The angle carries the message; this SOP governs how the teaching is staged.

## Anatomy

1. **The teacher:** one person teaching the viewer with confidence, expert or knowledgeable everyday person.
2. **The lesson:** the comparison, how-it-works, or myth being cleared up, shown with props or on-screen visuals rather than just talk.
3. **The product fit:** the product entering as the easy answer to the lesson, not a straight pitch.
4. **On-screen labels (optional):** text reinforcing the numbers, steps or comparisons being taught.

## Build rules (priority order)

1. Pick one thing to teach that your buyer gets wrong.
2. Show it with props or visuals, not just talk.
3. Keep the tone confident and simple.
4. Bring the product in as the easy answer to the lesson.
5. **Specific, real claims; no absolutes or banned phrases.**

## Length caps

| Element | Cap |
|---|---|
| Lesson | 1 main lesson, 2-4 supporting props/visuals |
| Product fit | final third of the runtime |

## Build steps

1. Read the brief: angle/misconception, teacher, props or visuals available.
2. Pick the one thing to teach that the buyer gets wrong.
3. Script the lesson with props or on-screen visuals (comparisons, numbers, steps).
4. Keep the delivery confident and simple.
5. Bring the product in as the answer to the lesson.
6. Claims pass (numbers and comparisons validated; no absolutes/banned phrases).
7. Deliver: teacher brief, lesson script, props/visual list, product-fit beat, claims flags.

## QA checklist

- [ ] One person is teaching the viewer something, with confidence
- [ ] The content is a lesson (how much, how it works, why), often with props or on-screen visuals
- [ ] The product fits into the lesson, not a straight pitch
- [ ] Honest claims; no absolutes or banned phrases
