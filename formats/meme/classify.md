# Meme — Classification Guide

(Definition from Alysha, 2026-09-30. Media: static or video.)

## Definition

An ad **made to look like an internet meme**: a recognizable meme template or meme drawing style (stick figures, "expectation vs reality", reaction images, captioned image macros), built to be shared, and **usually funny**. The product slots into the joke.

## The things that must all be true

1. **It reads as a meme first**, the kind of thing people send to a friend, before it reads as an ad.
2. **It uses a meme template or meme style** people recognize.
3. **Humor or relatability carries it**; the product is the punchline or the thing the joke is about.

## Quick test

Would someone share it as a meme even without the logo? If yes, Meme.

## Commonly confused with

- **An original drawing with a relatable caption:** not a meme unless the template or drawing style is one people would recognize from the internet. Relatable humor alone isn't enough; the meme has to be recognizable. Original hand-drawn characters go to the Creative Parking Lot as `doodle` (Alysha, 2026-09-30: Magic Mind crying brain).

- **Us Vs Them:** a straight comparison. A meme-style "us vs them" (like "expectation vs reality") is Meme.
- **Skit:** acted-out comedy on video. Meme is a template or meme-style image.
- **Comment Screenshot:** a real comment is the hero.

## Signals

Stick figures, crude drawings, top/bottom captions, lowercase dialogue, "expectation vs reality" split, a well-known meme setup.

Examples in the evergreen files folder (tagged `vf=meme`):

- Loop "Loop Dream vs Not Loop Dream", a real horse next to a crude horse drawing (the draw-a-horse meme)
- Olipop "you got the stuff? / yeah I got the stuff", stick figures over a briefcase full of cans
