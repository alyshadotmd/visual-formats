# Meme Format — Implementation SOP

## Reference examples (look here first)

To find examples, look in the shared `../../files/` folder for any file whose name contains `vf=meme`.

## What this format is

A static or video made to look like an internet meme: a recognizable meme template or meme drawing style (stick figures, "expectation vs reality," reaction images, captioned image macros), built to be shared, and usually funny, with the product slotted into the joke. It works because people share memes because they're funny or relatable, not because they're ads — a genuine meme earns reach and credibility a straight ad post wouldn't get on its own.

Strongest for **pattern-interrupt, top-of-funnel placements with audiences fluent in internet/meme culture, where humor and relatability earn attention an ad wouldn't.**

The one property to protect: **it has to read as a meme first, the kind of thing someone would send to a friend, before it reads as an ad** — and it needs a template or style people actually recognize, not just a relatable original drawing (an original drawing with no recognizable template is a different format).

## Inputs

Expect a **messaging angle** — the joke or relatable moment the product needs to slot into. The angle decides what the joke is about; this SOP governs picking a recognizable template or style and building the meme around it.

## Anatomy

1. **The meme template/style:** a recognizable internet meme format (stick figures, "expectation vs reality" split, reaction image, captioned image macro).
2. **The joke:** the setup and punchline the template carries.
3. **The product:** slotted into the joke, ideally as the punchline itself.
4. **The caption:** short, lowercase, meme-native text.
5. **Branding:** kept light so the share stays the point.

## Build rules (priority order)
1. **Start from a meme template or meme style your audience already knows.** A relatable original drawing isn't enough on its own; the template has to be recognizable.
2. **Put the product into the joke**, ideally as the punchline.
3. **Keep the text short and native** (lowercase, meme captions).
4. **Keep branding light.** The share is the point.
5. **Pass the no-logo test.** It should still read as a meme, and still get shared, even without the logo.
6. **Honest claims.** No absolutes or banned phrases.

## Length caps

| Element | Cap |
|---|---|
| Meme template/style used | 1 recognizable template per ad |
| Caption | short, meme-native (lowercase, brief) |

## Build steps

1. Read the brief: the joke or relatable moment the product needs to slot into.
2. Pick a meme template or meme style the audience already recognizes.
3. Build the joke around it, with the product as the punchline or the thing the joke is about.
4. Write the caption short and native (lowercase, meme-style).
5. Keep branding light so the meme still reads as shareable, not an ad.
6. Claims pass: claims specific and validated, no absolutes or banned phrases.
7. Share test: confirm it would read as a meme, and get shared, even without the logo.
8. Deliver: template/style chosen, joke and caption, product-placement note, claims flags.

## QA checklist
- [ ] It reads as a meme first, the kind of thing people send to a friend, before it reads as an ad
- [ ] It uses a meme template or meme style people recognize
- [ ] Humor or relatability carries it; the product is the punchline or the thing the joke is about
- [ ] Honest claims; no absolutes or banned phrases
