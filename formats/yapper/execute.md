# Yapper Format — Implementation SOP

## Reference examples (look here first)

To find examples, look in the shared `../../files/` folder for any file whose name contains `vf=yapper`.

**Whenever this SOP is triggered or referenced, watch the examples first** — the framing, the energy level, the pacing/cut rhythm, and how none of it looks staged.

## What this format is

A creator talking fast and direct to camera in a way that reads as a **spur-of-the-moment rant, a yap sesh, a FaceTime call** — something a real person just posted, not something a brand produced. One person, direct address, minimal setup, no b-roll crutch.

**This visual identity is constant no matter what the script says or which awareness stage it's written for.** Whether the argument underneath is agitating a brand-new problem or handling a warm retarget's last objection, the way it's shot and performed doesn't change: it always has to look like it was filmed in the moment, on a phone, by the person actually saying it. The script owns *what* is argued and *in what order*; this file owns *how it looks and feels* while that argument is delivered.

Strongest for **fast-moving hooks and objection-handling where raw, visibly real energy earns trust that a produced ad can't**, regardless of what awareness stage the underlying script is written for.

The one property to protect: **it has to look and sound like a real, unscripted moment.** The instant it looks lit, staged, or produced, or the energy sags, the format stops working, no matter how good the underlying script is.

## Inputs

Expect the **finished script** (the words, the argument, and the awareness-stage framing, authored through the scriptwriting skill) and a creator who can deliver it fast, direct to camera, and unscripted-sounding. The script carries what's argued; this SOP governs how it's filmed and performed.

## Anatomy

1. **The framing:** handheld or propped selfie-style, native phone-camera feel.
2. **The setting:** a real, ordinary environment the moment would actually happen in.
3. **The eye-line:** direct to lens, sustained throughout.
4. **The overlay/caption:** on-screen text for the opening beat, styled like something the person would slap on their own post.
5. **The product handling (if present):** held or used the way a person handles a thing they actually own.

## Build rules (priority order)

1. **Energy.** Fast, high, sustained end to end. No lulls, no dead air. This governs performance regardless of whether the underlying script is agitating a problem or closing a warm retarget.
2. **Framing.** Handheld or propped selfie-style, native phone-camera feel. Explainable in one phrase: held at arm's length, propped on a counter, set on a dash. If you can't say where the phone is sitting, it isn't native.
3. **Setting.** A real, ordinary environment the moment would actually happen in: a bedroom, a car, a bathroom mirror, a kitchen. Never a set or a backdrop built to look like nothing.
4. **Eye-line.** Direct to lens, sustained. This is a person talking to a friend through the camera, not reading a teleprompter past it.
5. **Lighting.** Reads native (window light, bathroom light, car interior), never an obvious ring light or studio rig.
6. **Continuity feel.** One continuous take, or jump-cuts that feel like breath/pause removal from a single real sitting, never a multi-scene edit that reads like a shoot.
7. **Text overlay convention.** A caption/overlay layered over the direct-to-camera shot for at least the first 3 seconds, styled like something the person would slap on their own post: native platform text (most often bold text in a solid white or black box near the top, above the face), normal sentence capitalization is fine, one native emoji is fine. Usually on screen for the first 1-3 seconds, then it disappears or hands off to running burned-in captions styled differently (smaller, lower, sometimes word-highlighted) so the two never get confused. This file governs that it belongs on screen and how it should look; what it actually says, including matching the overlay to the script's awareness stage, is owned by the hook skill (`../../../hooks/skills/yapper-hook-writing/SKILL.md`).
8. **Product handling.** If the product appears, it's held or used the way a person handles a thing they actually own, never presented to the lens or posed next to a screen. That reads as ad-brain and breaks the format instantly.

## Length caps

| Element | Cap |
|---|---|
| Opening overlay/caption | on screen for the first 1-3s |
| Edit style | single take, or breath-cut jump cuts only (never a multi-scene shoot) |

## Build steps

1. Get the finished script from the scriptwriting skill's yapper guide (the words and awareness-stage argument are decided there).
2. Pick a real, ordinary setting the moment would actually happen in.
3. Frame it handheld or propped selfie-style; confirm you can say where the phone is sitting.
4. Shoot direct to lens with a sustained eye-line, in one continuous take or breath-cut jump cuts.
5. Keep the energy fast, high and sustained end to end.
6. Add the opening overlay/caption styled like a real post, timed to the script's hook.
7. Handle the product (if present) the way the person actually owns it, never presented to the lens.
8. Burn in captions for the rest of the runtime.
9. Claims pass on anything said (specific, honest; no absolutes/banned phrases).
10. Deliver: framing/setting notes, performance direction, overlay timing, caption style, product-handling note, claims flags.

### Production handoff

Tight framing, direct eye-line, punchy cut rhythm if cutting at all, overlay placed and timed per the script's hook, high energy sustained end to end, captions on. Hand the script (from `../../../scriptwriting/skills/yapper/SKILL.md`) to whoever is filming — this SOP is what turns those words into something that looks real regardless of which awareness stage that script was written for.

## QA checklist

(Visual/production only — script QA lives in the scriptwriting skill.)

- [ ] Framing reads native (phone camera, explainable placement), not a rig
- [ ] Setting is a real, ordinary environment, not a set
- [ ] Direct eye-line sustained throughout
- [ ] Lighting reads native, not produced
- [ ] Edit feels like one sitting (single take or breath-cut jump cuts), not a multi-scene shoot
- [ ] Energy is high and sustained end to end, no lulls
- [ ] Overlay/caption present on screen for at least the opening beat, styled like a real caption
- [ ] Product (if present) is handled naturally, never presented to camera
- [ ] Captions burned in
