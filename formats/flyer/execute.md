# Flyer Format — Implementation SOP

## Reference examples (look here first)

To find examples, look in the shared `../../files/` folder for any file whose name contains `vf=flyer`.

## What this format is

A single printed sheet shown visibly taped, stapled, or tacked into a real everyday space — a hydro pole, community board, elevator wall, laundromat, storefront window, bus-stop pillar. It works because the handmade, non-ad look lowers ad recognition and buys a beat of curiosity before the sheet delivers the pitch; it borrows grassroots, "a real person printed this and stuck it up" authenticity, not the scale or spend of a paid media placement.

Strongest for **grassroots, scrappy, pattern-interrupt energy: challenger and underdog brands, community or local angles, and playful gags** (missing/wanted-poster jokes, lost-pet notices, band-flyer tear-tabs, apartment-for-rent notices, community-board announcements). Funnel fit is **TOF-MOF**.

The one property to protect: **it has to read handmade and stuck up by a person, not bought** — strip the placement and the whole idea deflates. Glossy or polished production pulls it toward the `ad-in-the-wild` format instead.

## Inputs

Expect a **messaging angle** — the product point to bend a familiar flyer trope around, and the offer or payoff it delivers. The angle decides the gag and the offer; this SOP governs the flyer trope, the staging, and the placement realism.

## Anatomy

1. **The flyer trope:** a recognizable real-world flyer format (wanted, missing, lost, free, for-rent, open-mic) bent to carry the product's point.
2. **The headline gag:** set big like a poster, up top.
3. **The product (small):** shown small as the "item/suspect/reward" inside the trope's world.
4. **The payoff line:** the offer, delivered in the trope's own language (e.g. "REWARD: 20% off").
5. **The placement:** the sheet affixed with tape, staples, or tacks into a real, textured space (pole, community board, elevator panel, laundromat, storefront glass), optionally with a hand or passerby for scale.
6. **The CTA:** stays inside the flyer's world — a tear-off tab, QR code, "scan me," or scrawled URL.

## Build rules (priority order)
1. **Pick the flyer trope that carries the message.** The recognizable real-world flyer format (wanted, missing, lost, free, for-rent, open-mic) is the vessel; bend it to the product point.
2. **Keep it to one printed sheet.** Handmade cues sell it: limited or mono ink, a slight skew, real paper texture, a headline set big like a poster.
3. **Stage the placement in a believable spot, true to where these flyers actually appear.** Pole, community board, elevator panel, laundromat, storefront glass. Add tape/staples and, when it helps, a hand or passerby for scale and realism.
4. **Keep the message-within-the-message structure.** Headline gag up top, product shown small as the "item/suspect/reward," offer as the payoff line.
5. **Keep the CTA in the flyer's world** — a tear-off tab, a QR code, a "scan me," or a scrawled URL. It keeps the illusion intact.
6. **Keep production unglossy.** Clean, paid-looking production pulls it toward `ad-in-the-wild` and kills the grassroots effect that makes flyer work.
7. **Hold to one clear headline and one offer.** Flyers get busy fast; let the placement do the interrupting.

### Variants (pairs well with)
The sheet's inner content can itself be styled as another format; tag that inner treatment as a secondary format when it's useful:
- **meme** (the trope gag itself)
- **listicle** or **product-grid** (when the sheet shows a lineup)
- **review** (a testimonial styled as a posted note)

## Length caps

| Element | Cap |
|---|---|
| Printed sheet | 1 |
| Headline | 1, poster-sized |
| Offer/payoff line | 1 |
| CTA | 1, staying in the flyer's world |

## Build steps

1. Read the brief: the product point and the offer to bend into a flyer trope.
2. Choose the flyer trope (wanted, missing, lost, free, for-rent, open-mic) that fits the gag.
3. Write the headline gag and the payoff line in the trope's own language.
4. Place the product small, as the "item/suspect/reward."
5. Stage the sheet taped, stapled, or tacked into a believable, real space; add a hand or passerby for scale if it helps.
6. Add a CTA that stays in the flyer's world (tear-off tab, QR code, scrawled URL).
7. Keep production unglossy: mono/limited ink, a slight skew, real paper texture.
8. Claims pass (offer specific and validated; no absolutes/banned phrases).
9. Legibility check at thumb size — one clear headline, one offer.
10. Deliver: trope, headline, payoff line, placement/staging note, CTA, claims flags.

## QA checklist
- [ ] One printed sheet, built around a recognizable flyer trope
- [ ] Handmade cues present (mono/limited ink, slight skew, real paper texture)
- [ ] Placement is believable and true to where this type of flyer actually appears
- [ ] Message-within-the-message holds: headline gag, product shown small, offer as payoff line
- [ ] CTA stays inside the flyer's world (tear-off tab, QR code, scrawled URL)
- [ ] Production stays unglossy, not paid-looking (else it drifts toward ad-in-the-wild)
- [ ] One clear headline, one offer; claims validated
