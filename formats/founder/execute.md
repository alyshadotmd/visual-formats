# Founder Format — Implementation SOP

## Reference examples (look here first)

To find examples, look in the shared `../../files/` folder for any file whose name contains `vf=founder`.

**Whenever this SOP is triggered or referenced, watch the examples first** — how much polish, what the setting communicates, and how the founder holds the camera's attention without performing.

## What this format is

A video of the founder talking on camera about the product as the person who created it: why it exists, how it was developed, and the mission behind it. It leans on authority and motive over production polish — a studio or a polished setup is completely fine, the credibility comes from who is speaking and what only they can reveal, not the setting. This visual identity is constant no matter which spine the script uses or whether the founder is fronting a standalone Founder ad or guest-starring inside another format; the way it's shot doesn't change based on the argument.

Strongest for **founder-led brand stories, product-origin and development reveals, and building trust or authority early with a cold or skeptical audience.**

The one property to protect: **the speaker has to genuinely be the founder or builder, with in-the-weeds detail only they could give.** Recast it with a hired creator, customer, or non-founder employee and it becomes a different format (EGC or yapper), not Founder.

## Inputs

Expect a **messaging angle** from the script SOP: the story, the belief, and the origin/mission argument, plus whether the founder is fronting a standalone ad or guest-starring inside another format. The brief also needs the founder cast as themselves (never a stand-in), and, where they exist, the specific prototype/workshop/notebook assets to shoot as b-roll against story beats. The script owns the story and argument; this SOP governs how it's shot.

## Anatomy

1. **The founder:** the real person who started the company or built the product, on camera, speaking as the originator.
2. **Setting:** either their real space (office, workshop, kitchen table, wherever the work actually happened) or a clean studio setup, matched to what the story needs.
3. **B-roll (optional):** cutaways tied to a specific story beat (the prototype, the workshop, the notebook) — never generic filler or a fix for a slow section.
4. **Captions:** on throughout.

### Beats (in order)
1. Founder opens on camera, eye-line direct, not off-axis to an interviewer.
2. The story unfolds with minimal cuts; this format earns trust through sustained, unbroken attention, not editing energy.
3. B-roll cuts in only at the exact moment it supports the story being told.
4. Performance closes reading like the founder explaining something to a friend, not presenting to a room.

## Build rules (priority order)
1. **It's always a video.** A static carrying founder copy is a different format (`letter`).
2. **Eye-line direct to camera**, not off-axis to an interviewer.
3. **Minimal cuts.** Trust comes from sustained, unbroken attention, not editing energy.
4. **Performance reads like explaining to a friend**, not presenting to a room. If it looks rehearsed, it undercuts the format's entire premise.
5. **Match the setting to the spine.** A workshop reads as evidence for a product-development reveal; a studio reads as considered and confident for a belief/manifesto spine. Setting is a supporting signal, never a requirement.
6. **B-roll only where it supports the story**, tied to a specific beat (the founder describing a prototype should have a shot of it, if one exists). If a section is slow, that's a script problem, not an edit problem.

## Length caps

| Element | Cap |
|---|---|
| Cuts | minimal throughout |
| B-roll | only where tied to a specific story beat |

## Build steps

1. Read the brief: the story, the belief, and the origin/mission argument from the script SOP, and whether the founder fronts a standalone ad or guest-stars inside another format.
2. Cast the actual founder/builder, never a hired creator or non-founder employee.
3. Choose the setting to match the spine: real space for a development-reveal story, studio fine for a belief/manifesto story.
4. Shoot with direct eye-line and minimal cuts; direct the performance toward explaining to a friend, not presenting to a room.
5. Note b-roll needs against specific story beats (if the founder describes a prototype, shoot it if one exists).
6. Claims pass (origin/product claims specific and validated; no absolutes/banned phrases).
7. Let the script set the runtime; production follows what the script needs.
8. Deliver: footage, setting note, b-roll list tied to beats, runtime, captions, claims flags.

## QA checklist
- [ ] It's a video of the founder speaking (never a static)
- [ ] Eye-line direct to camera, minimal cuts
- [ ] Setting matches the chosen spine (workshop for development reveals, studio fine for belief/manifesto)
- [ ] Performance reads like explaining to a friend, not a rehearsed read
- [ ] B-roll (if any) tied to specific story beats, not generic filler
- [ ] Captions on
