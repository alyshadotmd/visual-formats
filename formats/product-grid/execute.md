# Product Grid Format — Implementation SOP

## Reference examples (look here first)

To find examples, look in the shared `../../files/` folder for any file whose name contains `vf=product-grid`.

**Study one first** — notice that every image is a flat product shot and the whole layout reads as one complete set.

## What this format is

A static built **only from flat product images** (studio shots or cut-outs with no background), so the whole range snaps into a single satisfying set. The arrangement is flexible: uniform boxed cells, a varied-size mosaic, boxless labeled rows, or products scattered around a big headline. It works because a complete, orderly lineup signals range and abundance, and lets a viewer take in a whole collection in one scroll-stopping frame.

Strongest for **multi-SKU ranges, collections, sets and bundles**, and for the single-product play where the *same* product repeats per cell and a **label** carries the difference (days of the week, moods, occasions, use-cases).

The one property to protect: **only flat product images, reading as one clean, complete set.** Add a lifestyle or in-use tile and it becomes bento-grid.

## Inputs

Expect the **SKU/variant lineup** (or the single product plus the labels to swap per cell), clean isolated product shots, and any per-cell labels or prices. The messaging angle decides what the set is framed to prove (range, value, personalization); this SOP governs the grid.

## Anatomy

1. **Title:** the collection, set, or offer name.
2. **The layout:** flat product images only, consistent scale and lighting; cells, rows, mosaic, or scatter.
   - Optional center or mid-frame headline/offer tile.
3. **Per-cell label (optional):** name, price, day, mood, or use-case.
4. **Payoff:** brand lockup and/or the offer.

## Build rules (priority order)
1. **Flat product images only** — studio shots or no-background cut-outs. No lifestyle, model, in-use, or footage; that turns it into a bento-grid. A headline or offer tile is fine.
2. **Keep the product treatment consistent** — same lighting, scale logic, and background style — so the set reads as one.
3. **Show enough to feel complete.** A filled 3x3 or 3x4 reads as "the whole range."
4. **If one product repeats, let the label do the swapping** (days, moods, occasions) so each cell still earns its place.
5. **Pick one arrangement and commit.** Tight gutters for cells and rows; for a scatter, balance the products evenly around the headline.
6. **Honest claims.** Validate any prices or discounts; no absolutes or banned phrases.

## Length caps
| Element | Guide |
|---|---|
| Cells | ~6-12 (a full 3x3 / 3x4 reads as complete) |
| Images | flat product shots only (studio or cut-out) |
| Per-cell label | short (name, price, or one word) |
| Text | title, labels, and/or one offer tile; no paragraphs |

## Build steps

1. Read the brief: the lineup (or the single product + swap labels) and what the set should prove.
2. Lock a uniform cell treatment (crop, scale, background) and apply it to every product shot.
3. Lay out the grid; fill enough cells to feel like the complete set.
4. Add per-cell labels if the concept needs them (variant name, price, day, mood).
5. Add the title and brand/offer lockup.
6. Claims pass (validate prices/discounts).
7. Deliver: layout, cell treatment spec, labels, title, end lockup, claims flags.

## QA checklist
- [ ] Every image is a flat product shot (studio or cut-out) with consistent treatment
- [ ] No lifestyle, model, in-use, or footage images (else it's bento-grid)
- [ ] The layout is filled enough to read as a complete set
- [ ] Labels (if used) are short and consistent
- [ ] Prices/claims validated; no absolutes/banned phrases
