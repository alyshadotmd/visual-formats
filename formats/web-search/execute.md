# Web Search Format — Implementation SOP

## Reference examples (look here first)

To find examples, look in the shared `../../files/` folder for any file whose name contains `vf=web-search`.

## What this format is

A static ad made to look like an internet search: a Google-style search bar, search history, or autocomplete suggestions. The searches voice the viewer's problem, and the product shows up as the answer. It works because the searches read like the viewer's own thoughts, so the product arriving as "the answer" feels like a resolution rather than a pitch.

Strongest for **problem-aware audiences who have searched for a solution themselves, and "how to fix X" framings** (a hair concern, brain fog, finding the right sheets) where the product naturally slots in as the thing that answers the question.

The one property to protect: **the search UI has to look like a real search bar or history, and the searches have to sound like the viewer's own words, not marketing copy.**

## Inputs

Expect a **messaging angle** — the real problem or question the viewer would search, in their own words, moving from general to painfully specific — plus the product or benefit positioned as the answer. The angle supplies what's searched and why it matters; this SOP governs the search-UI staging.

## Anatomy

1. **The search bar/UI:** a Google-style search bar, search history list, or autocomplete suggestion rows.
2. **The searches:** 3-5 queries in the viewer's own words, voicing a real problem, moving from general to specific.
3. **The product:** shown directly under or beside the search as the answer.
4. **The tie line:** one short line connecting the product to the searches.

## Build rules (priority order)
1. **Write 3-5 searches in the viewer's own words**, from general to painfully specific.
2. **Make the search UI look real** (bar, icons, suggestion rows).
3. **Put the product right under the search** as the answer.
4. **Add one short line tying the product to the searches.**
5. **Honest claims.** No absolutes or banned phrases.

## Length caps

| Element | Cap |
|---|---|
| Searches | 3-5 |
| Tie line | 1 short line |

## Build steps

1. Read the brief: the problem/question the viewer would search and the product positioned as the answer.
2. Write 3-5 searches in the viewer's own words, moving from general to painfully specific.
3. Build the search UI to look real (bar, icons, suggestion rows), matched to the platform convention.
4. Place the product directly under or beside the search as the answer.
5. Write one short tie line connecting the product to the searches.
6. Claims pass: claims specific and validated, no absolutes or banned phrases.
7. Legibility check at thumb size.
8. Deliver: search queries, UI mock, product placement, tie line, claims flags.

## QA checklist
- [ ] A search bar or search results mockup is the hero
- [ ] The searches voice a real problem or question the viewer has had
- [ ] The product is presented as the answer
- [ ] Honest claims; no absolutes or banned phrases
