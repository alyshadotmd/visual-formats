# myth-vs-fact

Alternative names considered: `myth-buster`, `debunk-card`, `false-belief-vs-truth`. Name confirmed by Alysha: `myth-vs-fact`.

**Working definition.** Static split into two stacked panels: a MYTH panel (red prohibition / no icon) stating a common false belief or objection, and a FACT panel (green check) correcting it with a proof claim, often a study statistic, over a product shot with quick credibility bullets. The belief-correction contrast is the structure — one false belief, one corrective fact, stacked.

**Nearest existing format.** `us-vs-them` — but that format is a 4-7 row, two-column, brand-vs-competitor / old-way criteria table. This is one false belief debunked by one corrective fact (objection-handling), two stacked blocks, no criteria grid and no competitor. Also not `case-study` or `statistic` — a study stat rides inside the FACT panel, but the myth/fact framing is the format, not a standalone proof-number static.

**Examples.**

- **ARMRA Colostrum — "Ditch Your 10-Step Skincare Routine"** (static). MYTH: aging skin is just genetics. FACT: study participants noticed fewer fine lines in 3 months. Product shot with quick credibility bullets underneath.
