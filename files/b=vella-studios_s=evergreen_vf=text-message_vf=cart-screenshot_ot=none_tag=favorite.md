## Pre-analysis (creative analysis skill)

**Step 0: the ad's job**
- Entry point: desire. Wanting the class pack without feeling bad about the price.
- Persona: young women who love their Pilates but feel the cost, and who still get a little money help from home. The "treat yourself, someone else is paying" moment.
- Awareness: product-aware. It shows the exact product and price. The joke assumes you already know a Pilates pack is a splurge.
- Funnel job: entertainment-first, soft sell. Gets shared and commented on, keeps the studio top of mind, and quietly shows the product and price.
- Distribution: posted organically to followers. Run as an ad (Alysha's point), it would work cold in-feed too, because the joke needs no context and the product and price are already on screen. As a paid static it's a curiosity-click: the ad unit is clickable, so it doesn't owe an in-image CTA.

**Hook (static)**
- First thing the eye hits: the "Dad" notification, because it sits on top and breaks the screen.
- Then down to "(10) Mat Pilates Class Pack," then down to "Total $281.15." The viewer matches the numbers themselves.
- Tactic: relatability, with a confession feel (it reads like a screenshot from your own phone).
- Triggers: pattern interrupt (a real-looking phone screen, not an ad), identity call-out ("that's me"), and humor.

**Mechanic**
- The Implied Answer: nobody says "she's buying Pilates with the grocery money." You figure it out by matching $281.15 to $281.15. Working out the joke yourself is what makes it land.
- Layered with The Overheard Conversation: it looks like a private moment on someone's phone.

**Format**
- Text Message (iMessage notification) laid over a native app checkout screenshot.

**What's working**
1. The price is the punchline. The scariest part of buying, the total, becomes the funny part.
2. The odd cents ($281.15, thanks to the $11.15 fee) make it feel real. A round number would read as fake.
3. The product sneaks in for free: 10 classes, infrared and non-infrared, $270. Every viewer learns what a pack costs while laughing.
4. The caption adds a second joke ("emergency contact funds the emergency") instead of explaining the first one.
5. The comments prove people got it: "Girl math" is people repeating the joke back.

**What could be stronger (if run as an ad)**
1. No studio name or logo on the image itself. As organic, the account name covers it. As an ad shown to strangers, or when it gets screenshotted and shared in DMs, the brand falls off.

**Highest-leverage fix (additive)**
- A small studio handle in a corner, styled like part of the phone UI so it doesn't break the "real screenshot" feel.
